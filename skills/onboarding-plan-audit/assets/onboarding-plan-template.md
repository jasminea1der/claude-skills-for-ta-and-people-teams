## Your obligations for [Name]'s first 90 days

You are the largest single factor in whether this hire works. Most onboarding failures are a
manager who was busy in week one. Here is what is being asked of you, concretely.

### Before they start — by [date]
- [ ] Block your diary now: their first morning, a short daily check-in through week one,
      weekly one-to-ones from week two, and the 30, 60 and 90-day checkpoints. If you cannot
      protect this time, move the start date. That is a normal thing to do and a better
      outcome than a bad first month.
- [ ] Identify the first real piece of work. Small, completable in the first weeks, useful to
      someone. Not busywork, not a three-month project. Name who reviews it.
- [ ] Confirm access, equipment and accounts are set up **and tested** — not requested.
- [ ] Write the list of people they should meet in the first 30 days with the reason for each.
      You make these introductions; do not tell them to reach out.
- [ ] Choose and brief a buddy: a peer outside your reporting line, well connected, who
      volunteered and has time.
- [ ] Tell the team who is joining, when, what they will own, and what you want from the team
      in the first weeks.

### Week one
- [ ] Be there on the first morning. Personally.
- [ ] Ten-minute check-in every day. The point is availability, not content.
- [ ] Walk through the 30/60/90 plan and **agree it with them rather than issuing it.** They
      will improve it, and a plan they helped write is one they own.
- [ ] Agree the definition of "productive" for this role — one observable milestone you both
      sign up to. It is what the 90-day checkpoint measures.
- [ ] Hand over the first piece of work with the context and the reviewer's name.
- [ ] Tell them how things actually work here: who decides what, which meetings matter, how
      disagreement is handled, what "urgent" means, what gets rewarded.

### Weeks two to twelve
- [ ] Weekly one-to-one, protected. Do not cancel it in the first quarter.
- [ ] Feedback early and specific. A correction in week two is a conversation; the same
      correction in month four is a performance discussion.
- [ ] Ask for feedback in the other direction and act on something. "What have you not had
      that you needed?" is the most useful question you will ask this quarter.
- [ ] Run the 30, 60 and 90-day checkpoints on the dates already in your diary. Review against
      the outcomes in the plan, not against a feeling.
- [ ] Introduce them actively in the forums where their work is discussed. Being invited is
      not the same as being included.

### If you are away
- [ ] Name a deputy, brief them, hand over the plan, reassign the checkpoints rather than
      postponing them.

### Escalate early
- The plan is wrong for what the job turned out to be.
- They are materially off track at day 30 — raise it then, not at the probation review.
- Something they were told during hiring turns out not to be true.
