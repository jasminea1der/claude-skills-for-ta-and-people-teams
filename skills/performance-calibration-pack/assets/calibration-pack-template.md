## 3. Distribution approach

**The guidance:** [state the expected shape for the population].

**Applied at:** [company / function level — the level at which the sample is large enough
to be meaningful].

**This is guidance, not a quota.** It describes the population, not any individual team. No
manager is required to produce a rating they cannot evidence in order to fit a shape.

**The justification requirement:** any manager whose team departs materially from the shape
explains why in the session. That is what makes guidance more than a suggestion — and a
well-evidenced departure is an accepted answer.

**The rule that protects individuals:** no individual's rating is moved to fix a
distribution. The shape is a diagnostic that says where to look. The only legitimate reason
to change a rating is that the evidence does not support it.

**If challenged, the argument is:** without guidance, ratings inflate until the scale
carries no information and pay cannot be differentiated. With hard quotas, a genuinely
strong small team is forced to produce a rating that is untrue, and managers respond by
gaming the system. Guidance plus a justification requirement gets the comparability without
the injustice.

## 4. Manager pre-work brief

*This section is written to be extracted and sent to managers as a standalone document.*

### What to submit, by [date and time]

For each person in your team:

1. **Proposed rating**, against the scale.
2. **Written rationale**, referenced to the level expectations.
3. **Two or three specific pieces of evidence**, at least one from the first half of the
   period.
4. **Your answer to the two questions the room will ask:** compared to who, and what would
   have made this a higher rating.

### What a rationale that survives scrutiny looks like

- **Evidence, not adjectives.** "Consistently excellent" is not a rationale. Name the
  situation, what they decided or did, and what happened.
- **Referenced to the level, not to their own history.** The question is whether they met
  the expectations of the level they are at. Improvement is a development conversation; the
  rating is a standard.
- **Spanning the whole period.** At least one substantial piece of first-half evidence. This
  requirement exists because ratings otherwise reflect the last six weeks.
- **About work, not about the person.** Not their attitude, personality, manner,
  availability, health, or circumstances.
- **Written as if someone else will read it.** They may — an appeal, a future manager, a
  court. Factual, specific, professional.

### Language to avoid, and what to write instead

| Avoid | Why | Write instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Great attitude", "always positive" | Describes manner, not performance | What they delivered and at what standard |
| "Abrasive", "difficult", "too direct" | Characterological; applied unevenly across groups | The specific behaviour and its effect on the work |
| "Needs more executive presence / gravitas / polish" | Carries almost no evidentiary content and encodes similarity to the assessor | The observable behaviour to change and the business consequence |
| "Always available", "so responsive" | Measures hours, not performance | The output and its quality |
| "Has huge potential" | A different assessment, for a different meeting | What they did in this period |
| "Everyone loves working with them" | Popularity is not performance | The collaborative outcome and what it enabled |

### People on leave, reduced hours, or with adjustments

Rate the work done against the expectation that reasonably applied for the time and hours
worked. Pro-rate the expectation, not the person. Reduced volume from reduced hours is not
a performance signal. A disability-related adjustment is part of how the role is performed
and is not a deficit.

### The deadline

Submissions close [date/time]. The People team then runs the consistency checks and the
facilitator builds the session agenda from them — none of which is possible against a
midnight-before submission.

A person whose rationale is not submitted is discussed last, from whatever their manager
can say live, and the facilitator records that it was late. That is not a penalty; it is
what is physically possible in the time.

### What happens in the session

Your ratings will be discussed alongside those of other managers at the same level. You
will be asked the same three questions about every person: what did they do, compared to
who, and what would have made this a higher rating. Everyone is asked. A rating may change
if the evidence does not support it, and if that happens you will be told the reason and
supported on how to deliver it.

### After the session

You deliver every rating in your team, in a conversation, before it appears in any system.
You may say the rating was reviewed with other managers to make sure the standard is applied
consistently. You may not say the room overruled you — if you leave the session with a
rating you cannot stand behind, say so before the session ends. You may not disclose anyone
else's rating, anyone else's evidence, who said what, or your team's distribution.

## 5. Facilitator run sheet

### Timings

| Time | Item |
|---|---|
| 0:00 | Opening framing |
| 0:05 | [First contested case] |
| … | |
| −0:05 | Read back changes, final distribution, next steps |

### Opening (three minutes)

> We are here to make sure a rating means the same thing in each of our teams. That is the
> only purpose — not pay, not succession, not ranking teams against each other.
>
> The standard is the level expectations. For every name I will ask the same three
> questions: what did they do, compared to who, and what would have made this a higher
> rating. If I ask you those, it is not a challenge — I am asking everyone.
>
> A rating that cannot be evidenced does not survive this room, high or low.
>
> Everything said here stays here. And nobody leaves saying "the room knocked your rating
> down" — if you cannot defend a rating you leave with, tell me before we finish.
>
> [X] minutes, [Y] people. I will say aloud when I am confirming a name without discussion,
> and anyone can stop me.

Then the distribution guidance in one sentence, and what a departure requires.

### Per-person protocol

1. **Facilitator** states name, level, proposed rating.
2. **Manager** gives 90 seconds of evidence. Hold the time.
3. **Facilitator** asks the evidence questions.
4. **Room** — ask by name: who else has worked with this person and seen the work?
5. **Facilitator** states the decision and the reason aloud.
6. **Note-taker** records it.

### The evidence questions

- What did they do? *(then wait — do not fill the silence)*
- Compared to who? / Rank them and tell me what separates first from last.
- What would have made this a higher rating?
- What is the evidence from the first half of the period?
- What did they get wrong this year, and how did they handle it?
- Who else saw this work?

### Interventions

| Moment | Intervention |
|---|---|
| Cannot evidence a rating | Ask once more specifically → name the gap, not the manager → offer to bring it back at the end → if nothing comes, hold the standard and record the reason |
| Dominant voice | Take their view last on every name; poll on paper before discussing contested cases; "Hold that — I want the manager's read first"; offline at the break |
| Silent room | Ask a named person a specific question; assign a challenger role in advance; challenge a senior manager's rating first and let them answer it well |
| The trade | Interleave the running order; name the pattern neutrally; return to a settled name |
| Reopened decision | "New evidence, or new feeling?" — comparison to a later case counts, and reopens both |
| Potential offered as justification | "That is potential, and I want it — in the talent review. What did they do this period?" |
| Rating is a surprise to the person | Ask directly whether they know; do not soften the rating; support the delivery; flag if a formal consequence follows with no prior notice |

Bias interrupters: keep the in-room card from `references/bias-interrupters.md` in front of
you.

### Closing a decision

Say all three, then pause two seconds:

1. The decision. 2. The reason, in evidence terms. 3. "That is closed unless someone has
new evidence."

Where a rating changed, add that the manager will be supported on how to deliver it.

### Closing the session (five minutes)

1. Read back every changed rating and its reason.
2. State the final distribution and ask whether it looks like the population the room knows.
3. Confirm the communication timeline and the no-blaming-the-room rule.
4. Note what to fix next cycle.
5. Say what happens next and when.

### How to read the demographic cut

Its purpose is to surface a pattern for investigation. It is not a basis for changing any
individual's rating — adjusting an individual rating because of sex, race, age, disability
or any other protected characteristic is unlawful discrimination, including when done to
correct an imbalance.

Legitimate responses to a pattern: re-examine the rationales in the affected group against
the standard; examine whether the work allocation that preceded the ratings was equitable;
identify whether particular managers account for it; fix the process for next cycle. Take a
material or recurring pattern to counsel, and run the analysis at counsel's direction where
that attracts privilege.

Suppress any group small enough to identify individuals. Minimum group size for reporting:
[n].

### What a concerning result looks like

No fixed threshold. Judge on whether the gap exceeds the variation between other comparable
groups, whether it recurs across cycles, whether it concentrates in particular managers or
functions, and whether the rationales in the affected group are thinner or more
characterological. Recurrence and concentration are the strongest signals.

### Records and legal

- Rationales are evidence about work: factual, specific, level-referenced, free of
  speculation about personality, health, family circumstances or motivation.
- Every change carries a recorded evidence-based reason. A change without a reason reads as
  arbitrary later.
- Exceptions (leave, late joiners, reorganisation) follow a written rule applied to everyone
  in that circumstance.
- Anything approaching dismissal, a performance process with an exit path, or redundancy
  selection goes to legal and local employment law review before it is actioned.
