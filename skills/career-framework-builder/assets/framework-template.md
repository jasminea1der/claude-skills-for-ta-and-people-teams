months. If the top level is currently empty and intentionally aspirational, say so here.]

**What this framework decides.** Which level a role or person sits at, what is required to
move up, and which pay band applies.

**What it does not decide.** Performance ratings, individual pay within a band, bonus, or
whether a promotion is affordable in a given cycle. Those are separate decisions that use
this framework as an input.

**Levels are cumulative.** Each level includes the expectations of every level below it.

## 5. Titles and pay bands

**Level to title mapping**

| Level | Internal level | External title(s) | Band |
|---|---|---|---|
| | | | |

**Band principles**

- One band per level; the level determines the band, never the reverse.
- Adjacent bands overlap deliberately, so a strong performer late in one level can be paid
  above someone new to the level above. Pay recognises performance; level recognises scope.
- Position within a band reflects performance and time at level, not a partial promotion.
- Pay above band (red-circled) is used where an existing salary exceeds the band for a
  correctly mapped level. Pay is not reduced; future increases are adjusted until the band
  catches up or scope grows into it. Each case is documented.

**Promotion and pay are decided in separate processes.** Level people against the
descriptors first, apply the pay consequence second. Deciding them together lets budget
determine who gets levelled, which is the failure this framework exists to prevent.

**Regulatory note.** Pay transparency obligations in several jurisdictions increasingly
require objective, documented criteria for progression and pay, and may require disclosure
of ranges or criteria to employees and candidates. Confirm current obligations and timing
for [jurisdictions in scope] with counsel before publication. Nothing in this document is
legal advice or a compliance assessment.
